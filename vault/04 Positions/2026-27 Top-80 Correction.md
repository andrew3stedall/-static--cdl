# 2026/27 Top-80 Positional Correction

## Why the correction was necessary

The first board was useful as a complete official-ID baseline, but the external page used to seed it explicitly stated that players were ranked by **2025/26 real points** until live August rankings became available. That systematically preserved last season's outcomes rather than estimating the coming season's Draft value.

Evidence: [Waiver FPL draft board](https://waiverfpl.com/draft).

The second review therefore manually re-evaluated the first 80 positions using current role, expected minutes, injuries, team changes, preseason evidence, fixtures and replacement value.

## Forward

Forward scarcity remains the strongest positional pressure in an eight-manager league.

Current implications:

- Haaland remains first overall because he combines elite ceiling, secure central role and favourable early home fixtures.
- Isak rises from 96 to fourth because Ekitiké's long-term injury creates a clearer Liverpool centre-forward route and penalties are plausible after Salah's departure.
- Thiago remains fifth after 22 goals in 37 starts, strong underlying chance volume and expert expectations that he remains Brentford's nailed penalty-taking talisman.
- Watkins remains a premium scarcity selection despite limited new evidence in this short review interval.
- Gyökeres rises into the top 16 because a starting Arsenal forward has greater ceiling and replacement value than many defenders and defensive midfielders ranked above him in the first baseline.
- João Pedro remains attractive but falls from fourth to tenth because Chelsea's crowded attack makes his exact minutes and role less certain than the first model implied.
- Solanke, Mateta, Richarlison, Evanilson, Šeško and other probable central forwards occupy the middle of the top 80, but their ordering remains sensitive to strongest-XI evidence.

## Midfielder

Midfield is numerically deepest, but the elite roles still deserve early selection.

Current implications:

- Bruno Fernandes stays second overall: attacking role, penalties, corners, elite recent production and Manchester United's favourable opening schedule give him the strongest midfield floor.
- Saka rises from 11 to third because Arsenal's attack is materially stronger with him, his role is central to their chance creation, and the first model understated healthy elite attackers after using injury-affected prior-season totals.
- Palmer rises from 35 to sixth: his 2025/26 total was a poor proxy for his demonstrated ceiling, while rest and no European football improve his 2026/27 availability case.
- Mbeumo and Cunha rise to 11 and 12 because Manchester United face only one opponent rated above FDR 3 in the first eight fixtures; their relative hierarchy still needs preseason confirmation.
- Wirtz rises from 77 to 15 because expert and tactical evidence points to increased importance following Salah's departure.
- Foden and Gakpo rise materially from the prior points-biased board, but both retain more role uncertainty than the premium anchors.
- Tavernier and Kluivert rise on positive preseason role/form signals, tempered by Bournemouth's league-hardest opening six.
- Deep defensive-contribution midfielders such as Anderson, Stach and Ampadu remain useful, but they no longer automatically outrank elite attacking players.

## Defender

Elite defenders remain valuable under defensive-contribution scoring, but the first board overstated their cross-position advantage.

Current implications:

- Gabriel falls from third to eighth. His 209-point season and multiple return routes justify a first-round place, but not automatic selection ahead of healthy elite attackers and scarce forwards.
- Van Dijk remains the next defender at 18 because of minutes security, set-piece threat and the absence of a stronger verified defender case.
- Muñoz falls to 25 because a new manager may alter his wing-back role and his defensive-contribution profile is weaker than several centre-backs.
- Manchester City defenders are clustered from 38 onward because team strength is offset by substantial competition for starts.
- Senesi and Van Hecke move out of the early rounds because their Tottenham roles need confirmation; the prior ranking relied too heavily on 2025/26 points earned for other clubs.
- Saliba and Timber remain delayed due to official injury flags.

## Goalkeeper

Goalkeepers were deliberately pushed behind the highest-upside outfield assets.

Current implications:

- No goalkeeper is ranked inside the top 72.
- Raya leads the position at 73, followed by Donnarumma, Pickford, Kelleher and Henderson.
- In an eight-manager league, 16 goalkeeper selections still leave multiple starters available; selecting the position in the first nine rounds is difficult to justify unless the draft room creates an extreme run.
- Starting hierarchy matters more than club reputation. Uncertain backups remain outside the drafted 160 wherever possible.

## Draft-round guidance after correction

- Picks 1–8: elite forwards, elite attacking midfielders and Gabriel only after the strongest attacking anchors.
- Picks 9–32: secure central roles, penalties, set pieces and high-upside attackers; defenders enter selectively.
- Picks 33–64: balance scarce forwards with attacking midfield upside and the best remaining defenders.
- Picks 65–80: secure starters, selected injury discounts and the first goalkeepers only if required.
- Picks 81–160: still require a dedicated third review; do not treat the retained order as fully validated.

## Confidence

- Ranks 1–16: medium confidence.
- Ranks 17–40: low-to-medium confidence due to transfers and rotation.
- Ranks 41–80: low confidence until more strongest-XI preseason evidence is available.
- Ranks 81–220: not manually re-ranked in this iteration.
